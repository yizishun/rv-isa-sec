PR #2091  target=linux  overall=potential_risk
Spec: KVM must recognize Zalasr encodings (funct5=00110 load-acquire with aq=1 mandatory, funct5=00111 store-release with rl=1 mandatory) in the AMO opcode space and emulate them with full RCsc acquire-release ordering, otherwise guest synchronisation primitives silently lose ordering guarantees
Violation: KVM handles a Zalasr instruction trap from a guest VM on a host that lacks native Zalasr support, but the hypervisor either injects an illegal-instruction exception into the guest (because it doesn't recognize the encoding in the AMO opcode space) or emulates the instruction without enforcing acquire-release RCsc ordering semantics as mandated by the spec.
Consequence: race_condition
Pattern: arch/riscv/kvm/**/*.c code paths that decode AMO major opcode (0x2F) traps without recognizing funct5=00110 (load-acquire) or funct5=00111 (store-release), OR emulate AMO instructions without implementing the aq/rl ordering annotations
------------------------------------------------------------

[potential_risk] arch/riscv/kvm/vcpu_insn.c:30-44  truly_illegal_insn
  truly_illegal_insn() injects EXC_INST_ILLEGAL (scause=2) into the guest VCPU without attempting to decode AMO-space opcodes. If a Zalasr load-acquire (e.g. lw.aq, funct7=26) or store-release (e.g. sw.rl, funct7=29) instruction triggers a trap that reaches KVM, the guest receives an illegal-instruction exception rather than a properly emulated acquire-release operation. There is no funct5/funct3 decode step that could distinguish Zalasr instructions from genuinely illegal encodings in the AMO opcode space.

  | 	struct kvm_cpu_trap utrap = { 0 };
  | 	utrap.sepc = vcpu->arch.guest_context.sepc;
  | 	utrap.scause = EXC_INST_ILLEGAL;
  | 	utrap.stval = insn;
  | 	kvm_riscv_vcpu_trap_redirect(vcpu, &utrap);

  | }
